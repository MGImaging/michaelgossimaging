01 Desktop · spaces.media Phone

I founded this

Spaces Media

Problem

A Web3 radio station on X that has to stay on the air around the clock. Live Spaces drop, and every show has to be handed to the next host. Doing that by hand, day and night, does not hold.

What I built

I automated the schedule, the show handoffs, and the site. An operations agent, Orion, posts the daily schedule at 6 a.m., warns hosts 30 and 5 minutes before a show, and hands the broadcast to the next host. If the stream drops, a keep-alive brings it back. I directed the AI-assisted build and I test it.

Controls

A handoff counts only when the next host is confirmed as co-host. Routine health checks are plain scripts. Dangerous commands need my approval. Credentials stay in a vault the agent cannot read. Every incident becomes a written procedure.

What it does today

It broadcasts 24/7, with 25+ shows a week, as listed on spaces.media. My portfolio also lists thousands of hours on air and about 5.4k followers on X.

02 Desktop · botscoreboard.com Phone

Public site

BotScoreboard

Problem

Trading-bot sellers post screenshots. Those leave out losers, idle bots, and fees, so a visitor cannot see what a bot actually kept.

What I built

A public leaderboard of about 133 Polymarket trading bots. Each one is scored from its wallet’s public on-chain history, after trading fees, including rebates. Losing and idle bots stay listed. An automated pipeline refreshes the board. People can submit a bot, and there is a newsletter sign-up. I directed the AI-assisted build and I test it.

Controls

The methodology is published on the site. Each bot is labeled Confirmed, Self-claimed, or Suspected. The page states the sampling bias: bots taken from top lists over-represent winners. Only public data is used.

What it does today

The board is live at botscoreboard.com and tracks about 133 bots. The page shows when the data was last refreshed, and the methodology sits on the site.

03 — How I work

Spec, direct, test.

The same order every time. I do not ship a system I have not specified and then checked myself.

Spec

Every system starts as a written specification: the goal, the inputs, the success checks, and what it must never do. For a website that is the pages, the contact path, and who owns the domain and the logins. For a workflow it is the schedule, the checks, and a hard stop. Nothing sends a message or spends money until a person approves it.

Direct

AI coding agents build from that spec. I steer the work, read the pages and the code they produce, and send back anything that drifts. I design the system. I do not pass along a result I have not read.

Test

I open the result in a browser, on a desktop and a phone, before it goes live. For a running system I also check the logs and the approval gate. A send or a spend that skipped approval does not ship. When a test shows no edge, I report that. I do not loosen a limit to force a better-looking result.

04 — Standards

Work to code. Inspect before sign-off.

I work to code, and I inspect before sign-off. The same rule I use in the weld shop is the rule I use here.

Testing checklist

  • Phone and desktop checks at real widths
  • Working links and forms
  • A spelling pass
  • The page still works with JavaScript off
  • Reduced motion is respected
  • No horizontal overflow
  • A check in a live browser before launch

Limits on AI agents

  • They work from a written spec.
  • Their changes go on a draft branch, and nothing is merged without my review.
  • They cannot send messages or spend money without my approval.
  • They never get client passwords. The client keeps the domain and the logins.
  • They do not invent facts, reviews, or prices.
  • Placeholders are clearly labeled.

Approval rule

Any outgoing message or payment waits in a queue until I approve it.
